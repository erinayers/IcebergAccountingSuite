Privacy and data storage

  • Everything stays on your computer. Iceberg has no account, no cloud service, and no telemetry: it sends no data anywhere, does not collect usage statistics, and does not check for updates.
  • Your books are one encrypted file (.icb) in the data folder: %LOCALAPPDATA%\Iceberg\databases on Windows, ~/.local/share/iceberg/databases on Linux. Backups (backups/) are copies of that encrypted file.
  • Encryption: the database is encrypted with a 256-bit key in your .icbkey file using Fernet (AES-128-CBC with an HMAC-SHA256 signature), from the audited cryptography library. The key never leaves your machine and Iceberg cannot recover it for you.
  • Not encrypted: receipt images and invoice PDFs in the receipts folder you chose, CSV exports you create, and the log file (which records actions such as "Transaction 12 added: Office rent" but no bank account numbers).
  • The local interface is served to the window from a loopback-only address with a random port and a per-session token; nothing listens on your network.

Security guidance

  • Guard the filekey. Whoever holds the .icbkey can open the database. Store it separately from the .icb - a thumb drive, a password manager's file attachment, or an encrypted synced folder - and keep a copy in your backups.
  • Back up the data folder and the key. Iceberg keeps the twenty most recent backups, but they live on the same disk as your books. Copy the data folder and the key somewhere else regularly.
  • Download from the releases page only and verify the SHA-256 checksum before installing (see Verify your download).
  • Use your operating system's protections: a user account with a password, full-disk encryption (BitLocker, LUKS), and current updates. Iceberg protects the file at rest; it cannot protect an unlocked session.
  • Report security problems privately by opening an issue titled "Security" without details and asking for a contact, or through the repository's security policy if one is published.
